About

Tapas Tripura is a researcher in computational mechanics and scientific machine learning. He received his B.Tech. in Civil Engineering from NERIST and his M.Tech. in Structural Engineering from IIT Guwahati. He is pursuing his Ph.D. in the Department of Applied Mechanics at IIT Delhi, where he is affiliated with the Center for Scientific Computing and Computational Mechanics.

His research sits at the intersection of physics, computation and artificial intelligence: data-driven discovery of physical systems, operator learning, scientific foundation modeling, and reinforcement learning for intelligent control of mechanical systems. He introduced the Wavelet Neural Operator (WNO), proposed a Lagrangian discovery framework for uncovering governing equations directly from data, and developed the scientific foundation model Neural Combinatorial Wavelet Neural Operator (NCWNO). He is a recipient of the Prime Minister’s Research Fellowship (PMRF), awarded by the Ministry of Education, Government of India.

Research

Discovering physics from data

Governing equations are rarely known for real systems. This work learns them from sparse, noisy, output-only measurements by combining stochastic calculus, sparse learning and Bayesian inference. It covers stochastic differential equations driven by Gaussian white noise, partial and stochastic partial differential equations, and interpretable Lagrangians of deterministic and stochastically excited systems.

Predictive control without known physics

MAP-TD is a deep reinforcement learning method for controlling systems whose physics is unknown. A parsimonious dynamics model, discovered offline with sparse Bayesian learning, predicts long-horizon trajectories inside the agent, which improves sample efficiency and control quality. Demonstrations include deflection control of a cantilever beam, wind-induced vibration control of a 76-degree-of-freedom structure, and active flow control around a circular cylinder, with zero-shot transfer to a square cylinder.

Neural operators for computational mechanics

The Wavelet Neural Operator (WNO) blends the integral kernel of a neural operator with a wavelet transform. Wavelets are localized in both space and frequency, so WNO tracks fine patterns in the parametric dependence of PDE solutions. It has been extended to physics-informed training, multi-fidelity reliability analysis, tumor elastography and fault detection, and has been applied to forecasting 24 hours of Cyclone Amphan’s wind field from the previous 24 hours.

Scientific foundation model

Neural Combinatorial Wavelet Neural Operator (NCWNO)

Building a new machine-learning model from scratch for every scientific task takes a lot of computational time and memory. NCWNO is a foundation model for computational mechanics. It is pre-trained on a diverse spectrum of physics and then adapts to the solution operators of unseen parametric PDEs.

What it does

  • Learns solution operators for several parametric PDEs at once.
  • Generalizes to new parametric PDEs with minimal fine-tuning.

What sets it apart

  • It does not suffer catastrophic forgetting.
  • It keeps positive transfer to new parametric PDEs.
  • It transfers knowledge across dissimilar tasks.

In the showcase experiment, one pre-trained NCWNO predicts six PDEs simultaneously on a 64 × 64 spatial grid.
