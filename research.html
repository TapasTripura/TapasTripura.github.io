Discovering physics from data

Governing equations are rarely known for real systems. This work learns them from sparse, noisy, output-only measurements by combining stochastic calculus, sparse learning and Bayesian inference. It covers stochastic differential equations driven by Gaussian white noise, partial and stochastic partial differential equations, and interpretable Lagrangians of deterministic and stochastically excited systems.

Predictive control without known physics

MAP-TD is a deep reinforcement learning method for controlling systems whose physics is unknown. A parsimonious dynamics model, discovered offline with sparse Bayesian learning, predicts long-horizon trajectories inside the agent, which improves sample efficiency and control quality. Demonstrations include deflection control of a cantilever beam, wind-induced vibration control of a 76-degree-of-freedom structure, and active flow control around a circular cylinder, with zero-shot transfer to a square cylinder.

Neural operators for computational mechanics

The Wavelet Neural Operator (WNO) blends the integral kernel of a neural operator with a wavelet transform. Wavelets are localized in both space and frequency, so WNO tracks fine patterns in the parametric dependence of PDE solutions. It has been extended to physics-informed training, multi-fidelity reliability analysis, tumor elastography and fault detection, and has been applied to forecasting 24 hours of Cyclone Amphan’s wind field from the previous 24 hours.

Predictive digital twins

A probabilistic machine-learning framework for digital twins of dynamical systems that stay interpretable while predicting future states. It is also covered in the book chapter “Digital twin for dynamical systems” (Springer, 2023).
