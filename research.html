Research

My research is organized into three thrusts. Each one has methods and developments, and applications.

  1. Discovering physics from data
  2. Neural operators for computational mechanics
  3. Predictive control without known physics

Thrust I

Discovering physics from data

Governing equations are rarely known for real systems. This thrust learns them from sparse, noisy, output-only measurements by combining stochastic calculus, sparse learning and Bayesian inference.

Methods and developments

Stochastic systems discovery

Discovers the interpretable drift and diffusion terms of nonlinear stochastic dynamical systems driven by Gaussian white noise, using sparse Bayesian learning on output-only measurements.

Thrust II

Neural operators for computational mechanics

Neural operators learn the map from the parameters of a PDE to its solution. Wavelets are localized in both space and frequency, so wavelet neural operators track fine patterns in how solutions depend on the parameters.

Methods and developments

Wavelet neural operator (WNO)

Blends the integral kernel of a neural operator with a wavelet transform to learn the solution operator of parametric PDEs.

Thrust III

Predictive control without known physics

Learning effective control policies for systems whose physics is unknown, with high sample efficiency, which matters most where trial-and-error interaction is infeasible.

Methods and developments

MAP-TD

A deep reinforcement learning method for controlling systems whose physics is unknown. A parsimonious dynamics model, discovered offline with sparse Bayesian learning, predicts long-horizon trajectories inside the agent, which improves sample efficiency and control quality. Demonstrations include deflection control of a cantilever beam, wind-induced vibration control of a 76-degree-of-freedom structure, and active flow control around a circular cylinder, with zero-shot transfer to a square cylinder.
